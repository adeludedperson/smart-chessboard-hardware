The dark circles are copper keep-out zones — no copper pour near a hall sensor so the ground plane can't distort the magnet's field. The three tiny gold pads in each circle are where the sensor's legs poke through from the back. 16 resistors, 2 chips, 10 caps, 2 headers — that's all you solder on this side.

BOTTOM — faces UP, pressed against the wooden board

A3144 hall sensor (TO-92), one per square
sensor sits at the EXACT center of its square
U1's pins from the back
vertical routing lanes (B.Cu)
S12 label — matches square position
This side touches the underside of the wooden board. Each black D-shaped part is a hall sensor lying flat, face-up — it senses the piece's magnet through the wood. Note the mirrored numbering (S0 top-right here) because you're looking at the board flipped over.
How it works, one sentence: a piece's magnet trips the sensor under it → the two shift-register chips squeeze all 16 sensor states into a single wire → daisy-chains through J2→J1 across all 4 quadrants → ESP32 reads all 64 squares in ~64 microseconds over just 4 wires.

Why 4 identical boards instead of one big one: cheaper to fab, better yield, one dead board = replace a quarter not the whole thing, and each 180mm quadrant fits Lion Circuits' automated flow.

The one thing still missing before ordering: the real square size of your wooden board. Everything regenerates from that single number in ~30 seconds.
