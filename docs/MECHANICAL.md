# MECHANICAL — smart chessboard v5 "flipped sandwich"

**Scope: v1 sensing only.** No LEDs, no OLED, no acrylic. 64 squares, 4 PCBs,
one ESP32, wood. Nothing else is in this document.

**Model of record:** a parametric Blender model, every dimension a named mm
constant. The Blender sources are not in this repository; the renders are:
`renders/v5_assembled.png`, `v5_exploded.png`, `v5_cross_section.png`,
`v5_wiring_top.png`.

---

## 0. Do this first (2 minutes, before any wood is cut)

**The whole design assumes a magnet is detected through the BACK of the PCB.**
Nothing here is safe to build until that is proven on the bench.

1. Power a working board, watch the live dashboard.
2. Hold a 10 × 3 mm N35 disc **flat on the solder side** over S0 → must trip.
3. Slide a 4 mm MDF offcut between magnet and board (that is the real
   7.1 mm gap) → must still trip.

If step 3 fails, stop. The fix is a thinner lid (3 mm) or a stronger magnet, and
every number in §1 shifts. If it passes, the stack below is final.

## 1. Stack-up (z = 0 is the table top)

| # | Layer | Thickness | Top at | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rubber feet ×4 | **3.0** | 3.0 | Ø18 self-adhesive, one per corner |
| 2 | Base plate, 4 mm MDF 460 × 460 | **4.0** | 7.0 | sheet #1 |
| 3 | Cavity — M2 brass standoff ×16 | **20.0** | 27.0 | all electronics live here |
| 4 | PCB, FR4 | **1.6** | 28.6 | flipped: parts hang DOWN |
| 5 | Lead + solder relief gap | **1.0** | 29.6 | clipped leads on the "flat" side |
| 6 | Playing surface, 4 mm MDF 460 × 460 | **4.0** | **33.6** | sheet #2 |

**Total height 33.6 mm. Footprint 460 × 460 mm.**
Perimeter frame height = 20.0 + 1.6 + 1.0 = **22.6 mm** (base top → lid bottom).

Derived numbers the design lives or dies by:

| Quantity | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Magnet → Hall element | **7.1 mm** | 4.0 lid + 1.0 relief + 1.6 PCB + 0.5 die depth |
| Dupont housing hang below PCB | **17.04 mm** | 2.54 header block + 14.5 housing |
| Clearance under a seated Dupont | **2.96 mm** | 20.0 cavity − 17.04 |
| DIP socket + chip hang | **8.0 mm** | 3.5 socket + 4.5 chip |
| Sensor (bent flat) hang | **~4.2 mm** | TO-92 lying on its face |
| Piece base : square | **1.25×** | Ø40 mm base on a 50 mm square (FIDE ratio) |

The 20 mm standoff is chosen by the Dupont housing, not by the chips. A 15 mm
standoff would leave the housings 2 mm proud of the base plate.

## 2. Board geometry (ground truth)

Taken from `pcb/quadrant/chess_quadrant.kicad_pcb`, generated by
`pcb/generate_quadrant.py` at `SQUARE_SIZE = 50`. **The gerbers are ground
truth; where `pcb/DESIGN.md` prose disagrees, the gerbers win.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Board outline | 200 × 200 × 1.6 mm |
| Squares | 4 × 4 at 50 mm pitch, first sensor centre at (25, 25) |
| Mounting holes H1–H4 | (6, 6) (194, 6) (194, 194) (6, 194) — **6 mm inset** |
| Hole Ø | 2.2 mm (M2 clearance) |
| J1 | body at x = 4.5, y = 94.65, rot +90°, pin row runs inboard (+x) at 2.54 |
| J2 | body at x = 195.5, y = 94.65, rot −90°, pin row runs inboard (−x) at 2.54 |
| U1 / U2 (74HC165, DIP-16 W7.62) | centres (58.89, 101) and (158.89, 101) |

⚠ **6 mm, not 5 mm.** The build brief said the M2 holes are 5 mm from each
corner. They are at 6 mm. Drill the base plate to the 6 mm figure
(§5 coordinate table) and it will match the boards.

**J1/J2 pinout** (`pcb/DESIGN.md` §Connectors — read the silk before crimping):

```
J1 (IN)   pin 1..6:  5V  3V3  GND  /PL  CP  SER_IN
J2 (OUT)  pin 1..6:  5V  3V3  GND  /PL  CP  SER_OUT
```

J1 pin 1 is at the board edge, and the row runs inboard — so on a hop, **J2 pin
1 faces J1 pin 1** and the six wires run parallel without crossing. Straight
6-way ribbon, no swaps.

## 3. The flip, and what it does to the layout

Boards are mounted **component side DOWN**. The flat solder side faces up and
carries the playing surface. Consequences:

- Sensors sense through 1.6 mm of FR4 + 1.0 mm air + 4.0 mm MDF = 7.1 mm.
- Every connector, socket, chip and jumper is inside the cavity — nothing to
  route around above the boards, and the playing surface is a plain flat sheet.
- Clipped leads and solder fillets are on the *upper* face, so the lid cannot sit
  directly on the PCB. That is the 1.0 mm relief gap (line 5 of §1).
- **Top view = KiCad view.** Flipping mirrors x, KiCad's y axis points down;
  the two cancel, so board-local (x, y) from the gerbers is also the top-view
  (x, y) in the assembly. J1 stays on the left edge as you look down at it.

### 2 × 2 placement (origin = centre of the 400 × 400 active area)

| Board | Occupies | Rotation | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | x −200…0, y −200…0 (front-left) | 0° | chain IN from hub |
| **B** | x 0…200, y −200…0 (front-right) | 0° | |
| **C** | x 0…200, y 0…200 (back-right) | 180° | |
| **D** | x −200…0, y 0…200 (back-left) | 180° | chain OUT → ESP32 |

Rotating C and D by 180° puts every J1/J2 on one of two straight lines
(y = ±105.35), which is what makes the jumper runs short and parallel.

Chain: `hub → A.J1`, `A.J2 → B.J1`, `B.J2 → C.J1`, `C.J2 → D.J1`,
`D.J2 SER_OUT → ESP32 GPIO27`.

### Header positions in assembly coordinates

| Header | Pin 1 (x, y) | Pin row direction |
|---|---|---|
| A.J1 | (−195.5, −105.35) | +x |
| A.J2 | (−4.5, −105.35) | −x |
| B.J1 | (4.5, −105.35) | +x |
| B.J2 | (195.5, −105.35) | −x |
| C.J1 | (195.5, +105.35) | −x |
| C.J2 | (4.5, +105.35) | +x |
| D.J1 | (−4.5, +105.35) | −x |
| D.J2 | (−195.5, +105.35) | +x |

## 4. Cut list — 2 sheets of 4 mm MDF (Pilwal 610 × 610, 2-pack)

One sheet per big panel. They do not both fit on one sheet (460 + 460 > 610).

| # | Part | Size | Qty | From |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Base plate | 460 × 460 × 4 | 1 | sheet #1 |
| 2 | Playing surface (lid) | 460 × 460 × 4 | 1 | sheet #2 |
| 3 | Frame, front + back | 460 × 22.6 × 4 | 2 | offcut strip (150 × 610) |
| 4 | Frame, left + right | 452 × 22.6 × 4 | 2 | offcut strip |
| 5 | Lid locating block | 20 × 20 × 22.6 | 4 | laminate five 20 × 22.6 offcuts, or pine scrap |

Offcut per sheet after the 460 panel: an L of 150 mm width — a 150 × 610 strip
yields six 22.6 mm rips, far more than the four frame pieces need.

**Frame** = 4 mm MDF standing on edge, glued to the base plate perimeter, outer
face flush with the base edge. Front/back run full 460; left/right are 452 and
fit between them (460 − 2 × 4). Inner opening = **452 × 452**, giving 26 mm of
clear space on every side of the 400 × 400 PCB array — that is where the long
jumper run bows and where the ESP32 lives.

**Cut the back frame piece in two** with a 16 mm gap centred at x = −100 for the
USB cable (see §7), or drill/file the notch afterwards.

**Lid locating blocks** are glued under the lid's four corners, 4 mm in from the
lid edge (centres at ±216, ±216). They drop inside the 452 opening, so:

- the lid cannot slide,
- the lid's weight lands on the base plate, not on the PCBs,
- being exactly 22.6 mm tall, they *set* the 1.0 mm relief gap,
- no fasteners in the playing surface, and **no magnets near the sensors**.

The lid lifts straight off for service. That is the whole retention scheme.

### Playing surface markings

400 mm grid, 8 × 8 at 50 mm, centred. Paint/vinyl only — do not rout pockets,
every extra millimetre of wood costs magnetic field.

## 5. Drilling — 16 standoff positions

The four boards' mounting holes land on a tidy grid. In assembly coordinates
(origin = centre of the base plate) all sixteen are:

```
x ∈ { −194, −6, +6, +194 }   ×   y ∈ { −194, −6, +6, +194 }
```

- Base plate: 16 × Ø2.2 through, at the coordinates above.
- 4 outer corners at ±194 hold the array down; the cluster of 4 at ±6 supports
  the seam where all four boards butt.
- Feet: 4 × Ø18 rubber feet on the underside at (±212, ±212) — 9 mm from the
  edge, and clear of the M2 screw heads at ±194.

**Screw-head problem, and the fix.** The PCB→standoff screw head sits on the
playing side, 1.3 mm proud, into a 1.0 mm relief gap. Choose one:

- **A (recommended):** counterbore the lid underside — 16 × Ø5 × 2 mm deep at
  the same coordinates. Invisible from the top (2 mm of MDF left), and the lid
  still rests on its corner blocks.
- **B:** skip the top screws entirely. The boards sit on the standoff tops and
  are trapped by the lid. Fewer holes, but the array can shift a millimetre.
- **C:** M2 countersunk screws + chamfer the PCB holes. Not recommended on
  1.6 mm FR4 with copper near the hole.

Base-plate screw heads point **down**, 1.3 mm proud under the plate — the 3 mm
feet clear them by 1.7 mm.

## 7. Wiring — jumper lengths

All jumpers are **female–female Dupont**, plugged onto vertical pin headers that
point straight down into the cavity. Distances below are pin-to-pin in the plane
of the boards; the routed figure adds the 17 mm drop at each end plus a bend
radius.

| Link | Pin-to-pin | Routed | Buy | Qty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A.J2 → B.J1 | 9.0 mm (pin 1) … 34.4 mm (pin 6) | ≈60 mm | **20 cm F-F** | 6 |
| C.J2 → D.J1 | 9.0 … 34.4 mm | ≈60 mm | **20 cm F-F** | 6 |
| B.J2 → C.J1 | **210.7 mm**, identical for all 6 pins | ≈280 mm | **30 cm F-F** (40 cm comfortable) | 6 |
| hub → A.J1 | ≈286 mm straight | ≈350 mm | **40 cm F-F** | 6 |
| D.J2 SER_OUT → ESP32 GPIO27 | ≈112 mm | ≈160 mm | **20 cm F-F** | 1 |

Total 25 wires. One 40-way 20 cm F-F ribbon plus one 40-way 30–40 cm F-F ribbon
covers everything with spares.

Notes:

- The short hops are 9 mm apart but you cannot use 10 cm jumpers: each end drops
  17 mm below the board and needs to turn. 20 cm is the practical minimum and it
  coils flat on the base plate.
- The long hop (B.J2 → C.J1) runs up the right-hand side. All six wires are
  parallel and exactly the same length. Do not let the slack pile up under a
  board — bow it **inboard**, across the empty cavity floor, as drawn in
  `renders/v5_wiring_top.png`. There is only 2.96 mm of headroom under a seated
  housing, so slack goes sideways, never down.
- The 40 cm hub run doubles as the 5 V / 3V3 / GND feed for the whole array —
  the chain carries power as well as signals.
- Keep the 6 wires of a hop in pin order. J2 pin 1 faces J1 pin 1 (§2), so a
  straight, untwisted ribbon is correct.

### ESP32 + hub perfboard

Lives **inside the cavity** at the back edge, 70 × 50 mm perfboard centred at
(−100, +165), i.e. under board D. Height budget:

```
base top 7.0 → perfboard 1.6 → socket strip 8.5 → ESP32 PCB 1.6 → can ~1.5
= 20.2 mm absolute, i.e. 13.2 mm above the base plate
```

The nearest obstruction above it is a bent-flat sensor at 22.8 mm → **2.6 mm
clearance**. If that feels tight, solder the ESP32 down without the socket strip
and you get 8.5 mm back.

USB exits through a **16 mm notch in the back frame**, centred at x = −100.

## 8. Piece fit

- Squares are 50 mm. A 3.75 in Staunton king has a Ø40 mm base.
- 50 / 40 = **1.25×** — inside the FIDE recommendation (square 1.25–1.3 × base).
- Magnet: 10 × 3 mm N35 disc in a Ø10.8 × 3.2 mm pocket in the base, glued
  flush. Field crosses the 7.1 mm gap to the die.
- Sort every magnet's polarity before gluing — the sensor is unipolar
  (mark the working face, glue it face DOWN). A piece glued the wrong way round reads as
  an empty square.

## 9. Assumptions — read before cutting

Everything here is an assumption unless it is measured off a real part. Each one
is either a number the model needs, or a decision that could be made otherwise.

1. **Gerbers over prose.** M2 holes are at 6 mm inset, not the 5 mm quoted in
   the brief. Verify with a caliper on a real board before drilling the base.
2. **Magnet detection through the board back is unproven.** §0. This is the one
   assumption that can invalidate the design.
3. **Die depth 0.5 mm** below the TO-92 face (typical A3144). Not measured.
4. **Dupont housing 14.5 mm long.** Generic 2.54 female crimp housings vary
   14–15.5 mm. Measure yours; if it is >17 mm total hang, go to 25 mm standoffs
   and the total height becomes 38.6 mm.
5. **Relief gap 1.0 mm** assumes leads are clipped close and solder fillets stay
   under ~0.8 mm. If you solder before deciding, measure the worst blob and use
   that + 0.3 mm.
6. **4 mm MDF everywhere** because that is what is on hand. 3 mm lid would buy
   1 mm of field; 6 mm base would be stiffer. The lid thickness is the sensitive
   one.
7. **Frame is MDF on edge** (4 mm wide). It is glued, not screwed — 4 mm MDF
   edge will not hold a screw. Corner blocks (§4) give the glue joint help.
8. **Lid retention is gravity + 4 locating blocks.** No magnets anywhere near
   the playing field, ever.
9. **Standoffs in the section render** are drawn at the section's own y band for
   legibility; their true positions are the §5 grid.
10. **Wire routing in the renders is representative, not a harness drawing.**
    Lengths in §7 are what to buy; exact dress is up to you.
11. **ESP32 orientation** in the model (USB toward the back) is a choice. If you
    want the cable out of a side, move the notch — nothing else changes.
12. **Feet at (±212, ±212)** assume the board is only ever placed on a flat
    table. On a soft surface the 4 mm base plate will bow over 460 mm; add a
    fifth centre foot if that shows up.

## 10. Assembly order

1. Cut the two 460 × 460 panels and the four frame strips (§4).
2. Drill the base plate: 16 × Ø2.2 (§5) + the USB notch in the back frame.
3. Glue the frame to the base plate. Let it set. Check the opening is 452 square.
4. Screw the 16 standoffs down through the base plate from below (M2 × 6).
5. Fit the ESP32 hub perfboard at the back, cable through the notch.
6. Drop board A on its four standoffs, screw down (or option B). Repeat B, C, D.
7. Wire the chain: hub → A.J1, then the three hops (§7). Power up and run the
   64-square gate test **before** the lid goes on.
8. Glue the 4 locating blocks under the lid corners, counterbore if using
   option A, paint the grid, drop the lid on.
9. Stick the 4 rubber feet on.

Step 7 is the last point at which everything is still accessible. Do not skip
the full-board test there.
